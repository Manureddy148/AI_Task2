Round 4 (2026-09-04) — the documentation pass gets the same treatment

This document and its two siblings were hand-authored, so a three-agent release gate audited them the way round 3 audited the submission: a fresh-clone verification (passed all six steps — verify green from a clone, corpus hashes intact through checkout, overrides live, regenerated outputs byte-identical), a forensic docs-vs-ground-truth number audit, and a link/staleness sweep. The gate found 13 issues in the drafts — including a rounded "40×" where the machine output says ~38×, dates conflating the two working days, and the discovery that a round-3 bulk find-replace had silently rewritten the notebook's own historical record (the exact opposite of a lab notebook's job). All fixed; the corrections and the workflow-scale facts are recorded in NOTEBOOK round 4 so this page's every number is repo-traceable. GitHub Actions is green on every pushed commit.

The operator's role (the human, stated honestly)

This project was AI-executed under human direction — and saying so precisely is what makes it an interview asset rather than a liability. The operator:

  • Set and enforced the quality bar — rejected draft 1 ("professional enterprise level is what I want") and draft 2 ("not as per standards"), refusing to accept work that a staff panel would puncture; both rejections are what triggered rounds 2 and 3, where the most important defects (the unmeasured romanization claim, the 128k trap, the circular corroboration) were found.
  • Chose the escalation strategy — authorized the multi-agent adversarial review instead of another incremental polish pass; that decision is directly responsible for 30 verified findings.
  • Made the delivery decisions — corpus/scope direction ("architecture and intro files first"), when to commit, and the push to GitHub.
  • Owns the defense: the pre-submission checklist in AI_USAGE.md lists the re-derivations to perform personally (verify run, one live toggle, B1 on paper, one bench row by hand) — the repo is built so each is one command, and disagreement with any AI phrasing is itself evidence of ownership.

How to present this in an interview

  • The claim to make: "I directed an AI-heavy engineering workflow to evidence-grade output: every published number is machine-asserted (50 checks, in CI), every claimed bug has a one-toggle experiment, and when the output wasn't good enough I escalated to an adversarial multi-agent review of the work itself — which found 30 real defects, including two my own methodology explicitly warns about. Here is the incident log."
  • The claim not to make: hand-authorship of code that was AI-written, or debugging sessions that didn't happen. The repo's AI_USAGE.md is explicit, the git history is public, and the defense is live — honesty here is both mandatory and, given the process above, the stronger story.
  • The one-liner: the audit's method (measure, verify, adversarially review) was strong enough that it caught its own author — twice by hand, thirty times by machine.
